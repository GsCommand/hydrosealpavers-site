Paver sealing vs. paver resealing

Resealing starts with the old coating

Paver resealing is not simply applying another coat. A previously sealed surface must be evaluated for compatibility, trapped moisture, whitening, peeling, heavy buildup, uneven wear and joint-sand loss before a new system is applied. A sound maintenance coating may be cleaned and resealed, while a failed or incompatible coating may require testing, correction or stripping.

Faded or dry-looking pavers

Color loss and uneven sheen can indicate normal wear, especially on full-sun driveways and high-traffic areas.

White, cloudy or peeling sealer

Whitening may come from trapped moisture, coating failure, efflorescence, mineral residue or haze. Diagnosis comes before resealing.

Joint-sand loss

Open or shallow joints may need cleaning and ASTM C144 kiln-dried joint-sand restoration before the surface is resealed.

Our resealing process

Five steps before the new coating goes down

  1. Inspect
    Identify the surface, old coating, moisture concerns, stains, repairs and drainage.
  2. Test
    Check coating stability and determine whether cleaning, correction or stripping is required.
  3. Clean
    Remove organic buildup, dirt and loose material using surface-appropriate preparation.
  4. Restore joints
    Refresh joint sand where needed and complete agreed paver repairs or releveling.
  5. Reseal
    Apply a compatible contractor-grade system under suitable weather and moisture conditions.

Paver resealing in Nocatee

Nocatee surfaces face heavy sun, rain and irrigation

Nocatee driveways, pool decks and patios can experience reclaimed-irrigation staining, intense UV exposure, summer storms, high humidity, joint-sand loss and repeated wet-dry cycles. Those conditions can fade a coating or expose weaknesses in an older application.

HydroSeal evaluates the existing sealer before recommending a maintenance recoat. We do not automatically seal over whitening, peeling, trapped moisture or an unknown coating.

When stripping may be required

Full or partial removal may be needed when an old coating is peeling, soft, heavily built up, incompatible, moisture-damaged or too unstable to support another layer. Stripping feasibility and final scope require inspection and testing.

Learn why paver sealer turns white or see why sealer peels.

Resealing timing

Do not wait for complete coating failure

HydroSeal uses about three to four years as a practical planning range for many professionally prepared and sealed Florida surfaces. Full-sun driveways, frequent traffic, irrigation, pool chemicals, drainage issues, coastal exposure and harsh cleaning can shorten service life.

Annual inspection can be useful, but annual recoating is not automatically recommended. Recoating too frequently can create unnecessary film buildup or bury an unstable layer.

Driveway sealing FAQs

Common questions before sealing a driveway.

How much does driveway paver sealing cost?

Pricing depends on square footage, surface condition, joint stability, staining, access, previous coatings, and preparation requirements. Use the pricing calculator for a planning estimate or request a current project quote.

How long before vehicles can return after sealing?

Vehicle return commonly requires about 48–72 hours, but actual cure timing depends on the sealer, temperature, humidity, shade, surface condition, and weather. Follow the cure guidance provided for the specific project.

Do you restore joint sand on driveway projects?

When joint-sand installation or restoration is needed, it is included in the project scope. The amount of joint work depends on existing joint depth, washout, stability, cleaning requirements, and surface condition.

Can old or failed sealer simply be coated over?

Not always. White haze, peeling, incompatible coatings, trapped moisture, or unstable existing sealer can require additional evaluation or stripping before a new coating is appropriate.

Will sealing remove every oil, rust, or irrigation stain?

No. Professional cleaning and targeted treatment can improve many stains, but complete removal is not guaranteed. Some discoloration can be permanent or require separate restoration work.

What if the driveway has sinking pavers or drainage problems?

Those conditions should be evaluated before sealing. A protective coating does not repair an unstable base or correct drainage problems by itself.
