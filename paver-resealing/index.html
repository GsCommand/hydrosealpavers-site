Paver sealing vs. paver resealing

Resealing starts with the old coating

Paver resealing is not simply applying another coat. A previously sealed surface must be evaluated for compatibility, trapped moisture, whitening, peeling, heavy buildup, uneven wear and joint-sand loss before a new system is applied. A sound maintenance coating may be cleaned and resealed, while a failed or incompatible coating may require testing, correction or stripping.

Faded or dry-looking pavers

Color loss and uneven sheen can indicate normal wear, especially on full-sun driveways and high-traffic areas.

White, cloudy or peeling sealer

Whitening may come from trapped moisture, coating failure, efflorescence, mineral residue or haze. Diagnosis comes before resealing.

Joint-sand loss

Open or shallow joints may need cleaning and ASTM C144 kiln-dried joint-sand restoration before the surface is resealed.

Our resealing process

Five steps before the new coating goes down

  1. Inspect
    Identify the surface, old coating, moisture concerns, stains, repairs and drainage.
  2. Test
    Check coating stability and determine whether cleaning, correction or stripping is required.
  3. Clean
    Remove organic buildup, dirt and loose material using surface-appropriate preparation.
  4. Restore joints
    Refresh joint sand where needed and complete agreed paver repairs or releveling.
  5. Reseal
    Apply a compatible contractor-grade system under suitable weather and moisture conditions.

When stripping may be required

Full or partial removal may be needed when an old coating is peeling, soft, heavily built up, incompatible, moisture-damaged or too unstable to support another layer. Stripping feasibility and final scope require inspection and testing.

Resealing timing

Do not wait for complete coating failure

HydroSeal uses about three to four years as a practical planning range for many professionally prepared and sealed Florida surfaces. Full-sun driveways, frequent traffic, irrigation, pool chemicals, drainage issues, coastal exposure and harsh cleaning can shorten service life.

Annual inspection can be useful, but annual recoating is not automatically recommended. Recoating too frequently can create unnecessary film buildup or bury an unstable layer.

Paver resealing FAQs

Common questions before resealing

How do I know when my pavers need resealing?

Look for faded color, reduced water resistance, uneven sheen, joint-sand loss, increased staining or a dry, worn appearance. White, peeling or soft areas may signal failure rather than normal wear.

Can you reseal over old paver sealer?

Sometimes. The old coating must be clean, stable and compatible. Unknown, whitening, peeling or heavily built-up coatings may require testing or removal first.

How much does paver resealing cost?

Cost depends on square footage, surface type, coating condition, stripping, joint sand, repairs, stains and access. Use the paver sealing cost calculator for a preliminary range.

Can failed sealer always be removed?

Not always completely or in one treatment. Results depend on sealer chemistry, number of layers, paver porosity, moisture and surface condition. Feasibility requires inspection.
