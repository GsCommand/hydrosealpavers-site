Jacksonville Joint-Sand Cost Guide

Paver Resanding Cost in Jacksonville, FL

Resanding price depends on more than square footage. Joint depth, sand loss, contamination, removal work, drainage, paver movement and whether the project includes cleaning and sealing all change the scope.

Written by HydroSeal Editorial Team · Field reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified team · Last technically reviewed August 11, 2026

Quick answer

A reliable paver-resanding quote requires measuring the area and inspecting the joints. Two 1,000-square-foot surfaces can require very different amounts of labor and material if one has shallow clean joints and the other has weeds, old polymeric material, erosion, settlement or deeply empty joints.

10 factors that change resanding cost

  1. Total measured paver area
  2. Joint width and average joint depth
  3. Amount of missing sand
  4. Weeds, roots, ants and organic contamination
  5. Old polymeric or hardened joint material
  6. Cleaning and removal required before refill
  7. Drying time before sand installation
  8. Drainage and runoff that caused previous washout
  9. Loose, rocking, spreading or sunken pavers
  10. Whether sealing is included after resanding

Why cheap resanding can fail

  • New sand is swept over debris instead of into adequately prepared joints.
  • Joints are not deep enough to hold material.
  • Drainage or runoff that caused washout is never corrected.
  • Loose pavers are ignored.
  • Sand is installed while joints remain wet.
  • The wrong joint material is used for the surface and project conditions.

What should a resanding quote include?

  • Measured square footage
  • Joint condition and removal assumptions
  • Cleaning scope
  • Sand type and color
  • Areas excluded from refill
  • Repair or re-leveling items
  • Drying requirements
  • Whether sealing is included
  • Limitations for weeds, ants and future washout

Common questions

What determines paver resanding cost?

Measured area, joint dimensions, removal work, contamination, drainage, repairs, access and whether cleaning or sealing is included.

Can new sand simply be swept over old sand?

Not always. Shallow, contaminated or hardened joints may require preparation or removal before refill.

Should pavers be sealed after resanding?

Sometimes. Sealing may help stabilize compatible joint sand when the surface and product system are suitable, but it is a separate scope decision.
