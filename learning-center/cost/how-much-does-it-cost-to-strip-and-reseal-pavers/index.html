Paver Restoration Cost

How Much Does It Cost to Strip and Reseal Pavers?

Removing failed sealer is a restoration project, not a routine maintenance coat. The price depends far more on coating failure and removal difficulty than on square footage alone.

Updated August 9, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Quick answer: In Northeast Florida, strip-and-reseal work normally costs substantially more than standard cleaning, sanding, and resealing because the failed coating must be removed before the surface can be rebuilt. A firm price requires measurements, a coating inspection, and often a test area. Square-foot pricing alone is unreliable when the old sealer is cloudy, peeling, thick, chemically resistant, or uneven.

Standard resealing assumes the existing surface is sound enough to clean, prepare, and accept another compatible coat. Stripping changes the project. The contractor must determine what coating is present, how strongly it is bonded, whether moisture is trapped beneath it, and whether removal chemicals can be used safely around landscaping, pool water, walls, drains, and adjacent materials.

When is stripping actually necessary?

Stripping is usually considered when the old sealer is peeling, whitening, milky, patchy, heavily built up, incompatible with the proposed product, or trapping moisture. It may also be needed when a previous coating has darkened the pavers unevenly or created slick, over-applied areas.

Do not assume every faded driveway needs complete removal. If the existing coating is still bonded and chemically compatible, a professional may recommend cleaning, preparation, joint-sand restoration, and a controlled recoat. That is why the first decision should be cleaning, resealing, or stripping—not automatically choosing the most aggressive option.

What should a proper quote include?

A credible proposal should separate the restoration steps instead of hiding everything under “seal pavers.” Look for measured square footage, test-area findings, expected number of removal passes, stain or efflorescence limitations, joint-sand scope, repairs, sealer system, cure restrictions, and what happens if the existing coating proves harder to remove than expected.

HydroSeal does not promise that every stain, mineral deposit, silicone mark, haze, or old coating will disappear completely. Some defects are inside the paver, beneath the coating, or permanently embedded. The purpose of a test area is to show what improvement is realistically achievable before the full project proceeds.

How to control the final cost

The cheapest time to address a failing coating is before another incompatible layer is added. Avoid repeated “quick reseals” over cloudy or peeling surfaces. Keep sprinklers off the work area, remove furniture before the project, disclose previous products when known, and allow enough time for proper drying. Those steps do not eliminate restoration costs, but they reduce avoidable delays and surprises.

Frequently asked questions

Does every resealing project require stripping?

No. Stripping is normally reserved for failed, incompatible, heavily built-up, peeling, cloudy, or uneven coatings. Sound existing sealer may sometimes be cleaned, tested for compatibility, and recoated.

Why does strip-and-reseal cost more than normal resealing?

It adds chemical removal, controlled dwell time, agitation, recovery, repeated rinsing, drying, compatibility testing, and often additional joint-sand work before new sealer can be applied.

Can a strip-and-reseal price be confirmed from photos?

Photos can identify obvious failure patterns, but a reliable scope usually requires measurements and an on-site test area because coating thickness, chemistry, moisture, access, and removal difficulty vary.
