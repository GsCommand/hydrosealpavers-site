Paver Problems

Why Is My Paver Sealer Peeling?

Paver sealer peels when the coating loses adhesion because of trapped moisture, poor preparation, incompatible layers, contamination, or excessive film build.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

What peeling means

Peeling, flaking, or sheets lifting from the pavers indicate that the coating is no longer bonded. The failure may begin at edges, low areas, shade lines, hot-tire zones, or places where old coating was weakest. A peeling film cannot be made sound by applying more sealer on top.

The most common causes

Sealing damp pavers is a leading cause in Florida. Other causes include coating over dirt or efflorescence, applying incompatible products, sealing over unstable old film, and using coats that are too heavy. Oil, tire residue, silicone, paint, and cleaning chemicals can also interfere with adhesion.

Why failure appears later

A surface may look good on completion day. Heat expands the pavers, rain adds moisture, and vapor pressure stresses the bond over weeks or months. That delayed failure is why weather, dry time, and surface testing matter more than same-day shine.

How it is corrected

Remove loose and failed coating. Spot repairs may work when the failure is isolated and the remaining film is stable, but widespread delamination usually requires full stripping. After removal, rinse thoroughly, allow complete dry time, test compatibility, and apply the new system at the specified coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Can pressure washing remove peeling sealer?

It may remove loose film but rarely removes bonded coating evenly.

Can I reseal only the peeling spots?

Sometimes, but color and sheen may differ. Widespread failure usually needs a full reset.

Is peeling always covered by warranty?

No. Coverage depends on workmanship terms, drainage, chemical exposure, and maintenance exclusions.
