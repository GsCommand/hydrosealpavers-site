Paver Problems

Why Is My Paver Sealer Peeling?

Peeling means the sealer film is no longer bonded to the paver surface. In Florida, the usual causes are moisture, inadequate preparation, product incompatibility, excessive application, or recoating over a weak existing layer.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

The direct answer

A sealer can look acceptable immediately after application and still fail later. Heat, rain, irrigation, traffic, and repeated moisture cycling expose weak adhesion. The failure may begin as small flakes around joints or shaded areas, then spread into larger patches.

The most common causes

Trapped moisture

Pavers may feel dry while retaining water below the surface. Once sealed, heat pushes that moisture upward and can create whitening, bubbles, or loss of adhesion.

Insufficient preparation

Algae, old sealer, cleaner residue, efflorescence, sand dust, oil, and other contamination prevent the new coating from bonding directly to the paver.

Recoating a failing layer

A new coat cannot repair a weak foundation. When old sealer is already peeling or chalking, the fresh material eventually comes off with it.

Incompatible products

Unknown or mismatched water-based and solvent-based systems may not bond correctly. Product history matters before a maintenance coat is applied.

Too much sealer

Heavy coats can create excessive film build, slow internal curing, trap solvent or moisture, and leave a coating that fails under Florida conditions.

Peeling, whitening, and efflorescence are different

Can another coat fix it?

Usually not. Another coat may temporarily darken the surface, but it does not restore adhesion underneath and can make later stripping more difficult.

What a proper correction involves

  1. Identify the existing coating and extent of failure.
  2. Check drainage, irrigation, shade, and moisture patterns.
  3. Remove loose or failed sealer.
  4. Clean and rinse thoroughly.
  5. Allow complete drying.
  6. Apply a compatible breathable system at the correct rate.

Frequently asked questions

Why did it peel only in certain areas?

Those areas may hold more moisture, receive heavier traffic, contain contamination, or have thicker coating buildup.

Can pressure washing remove peeling sealer?

Pressure may remove loose fragments but rarely creates a uniform prepared surface and can damage joints.

Does all peeling require a full strip?

No. The decision depends on how much coating failed and whether the remaining film is firmly bonded.
