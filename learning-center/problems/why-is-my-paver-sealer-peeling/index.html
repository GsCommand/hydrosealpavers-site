Paver-Sealer Failure Guide

Why Is My Paver Sealer Peeling in Jacksonville?

Peeling, flaking, bubbling, or sheets lifting from pavers means the coating has lost adhesion. The cause is usually moisture, poor preparation, incompatible layers, contamination, excessive film, or an unstable old coating.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Peeling paver sealer cannot normally be repaired by adding another coat. The failed layer must be diagnosed first. Loose film may need spot removal, while widespread delamination often requires stripping, rinsing, drying, compatibility testing, and a complete reset.

7 common causes of peeling paver sealer

  1. Trapped moisture: damp pavers, wet joints, irrigation, or vapor pressure weaken adhesion.
  2. Poor surface preparation: dirt, algae, residue, or unstable material remains beneath the coating.
  3. Incompatible old sealer: the new system cannot bond to the existing chemistry.
  4. Excessive film build: repeated or heavy coats create a thick layer that moves differently from the paver.
  5. Oil, silicone, tire residue, or chemicals: contamination blocks proper bonding.
  6. Unsafe weather or cure conditions: rain, dew, humidity, or temperature changes interrupt curing.
  7. Weak old coating: the new sealer bonds to a layer that was already failing.

Why Jacksonville conditions accelerate failure

Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, Jacksonville Beach, and nearby Northeast Florida communities combine humidity, irrigation, afternoon rain, overnight dew, full sun, shaded pool enclosures, and coastal moisture. These conditions create repeated wet-dry and heat cycles that expose weak preparation or trapped moisture over time.

Peeling may not appear on completion day. A coating can look uniform at first, then fail after weeks or months of rain, heat, vehicle traffic, and vapor movement.

What not to do

  • Do not bury peeling film under another coat.
  • Do not assume pressure washing alone will remove all failed coating evenly.
  • Do not use unknown solvents without testing the paver and coating.
  • Do not promise complete coating removal before evaluating the surface.
  • Do not reseal until the pavers, joints, drainage, and old coating are understood.

Common questions

Can another coat fix peeling paver sealer?

Usually not. Peeling means the existing coating has lost adhesion. Adding more product can bury the unstable layer and make removal more difficult.

Can pressure washing remove peeling paver sealer?

Pressure washing may remove loose flakes but rarely removes bonded coating evenly. Complete correction often requires testing and a controlled stripping process.

Does all peeling paver sealer require full stripping?

Not always. Isolated failure may allow a limited repair if the surrounding coating is stable and compatible, but widespread delamination usually requires a full reset.
