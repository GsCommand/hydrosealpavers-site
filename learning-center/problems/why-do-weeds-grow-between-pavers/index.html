Paver Problem Guide

Why Do Weeds Grow Between Pavers?

Weeds between pavers do not automatically mean roots are growing up from deep below. Seeds often germinate in organic debris collected in open or deteriorated joints.

Updated August 7, 2026 by HydroSeal

Direct answer

Weeds commonly grow when wind-blown seeds settle into joints containing moisture, dirt and organic material. Low joint sand, erosion and neglected maintenance create more room for growth. Some weeds can emerge from below, but surface germination is common.

Why paver joints become weed-friendly

  • Joint sand has washed out or settled low.
  • Organic debris accumulates in the joint.
  • Irrigation or shade keeps the area damp.
  • Algae and soil create a growing medium.
  • Edges or drainage allow recurring contamination.

Will resanding stop weeds forever?

No. Restoring full joints reduces open space and can make maintenance easier, but it does not make the paver system biologically sterile. Seeds can land on top again and germinate in debris.

Does sealer prevent weeds?

A compatible stabilizing sealer can help bind the upper joint and reduce sand loss in appropriate systems, but no responsible contractor should promise permanent weed prevention. Ongoing cleaning and control of moisture and debris still matter.

Best sequence for weeded pavers

Remove active growth and debris, clean the surface and joints appropriately, evaluate sand depth, allow the system to dry, restore joint sand where needed, then seal only if the surface and coating history are suitable.
