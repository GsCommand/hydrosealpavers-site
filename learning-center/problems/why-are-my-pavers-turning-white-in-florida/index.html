Paver Problems

Why Are My Pavers Turning White in Florida?

White pavers in Florida usually indicate efflorescence, trapped moisture in sealer, failed coating, or cleaning residue. The correct repair depends on which one is present.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

The four common causes

Efflorescence leaves mineral deposits as water evaporates. Sealer blushing creates a milky appearance when moisture interferes with a film. Peeling or delamination exposes white edges and patches. Cleaner or hard-water residue can also dry on the surface. More than one condition can exist at the same time.

How to diagnose the white area

Powder that brushes off points toward efflorescence. A white patch that changes as the surface warms or dries may involve moisture in the coating. Flakes, sharp edges, or lifted film indicate adhesion failure. Check whether the area follows irrigation patterns, low spots, shade lines, furniture, or old coating boundaries.

Why sealing over it is risky

Clear sealer does not make every white deposit disappear. It can darken residue, trap moisture, and create permanent-looking contrast. The surface should be cleaned or stripped according to the cause, rinsed, dried, and tested before recoating.

Repair paths

Efflorescence needs mineral treatment. Cleaner residue needs thorough rinsing. Persistent sealer haze or peeling may require partial or full stripping. After correction, drainage, irrigation, dry time, compatible product, and controlled application reduce repeat failure.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my pavers turn white after rain?

Rain may reveal trapped moisture in sealer or move salts to the surface.

Will the haze go away by itself?

Temporary moisture haze may improve, but persistent film failure or mineral deposits usually need treatment.

Can another coat fix it?

Usually not without diagnosis. Another coat may worsen trapped moisture or buildup.
