Paver Problems

Sealed pavers are usually slippery because the surface has too much sealer film, the wrong type of sealer was used, traction additive was omitted, or algae and residue have built up on top. The correct fix depends on whether the problem started immediately after sealing or developed later.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

The quick diagnosis

If the pavers became slippery as soon as the sealer cured, the most likely causes are excessive sealer, a high-gloss film on a smooth surface, or insufficient traction additive. If the surface was safe at first and became slippery months later, organic film, sunscreen, irrigation residue, shade, or worn texture is more likely.

Pool decks deserve extra caution because bare feet, standing water, sunscreen, and frequent moisture make small traction problems more noticeable than they would be on a driveway.

Common reasons sealed pavers become slippery

Too much sealer was applied

Film-forming sealers are designed to leave a controlled layer at the surface. Heavy coats, pooled material, repeated resealing without proper preparation, or applying a second coat before the first has set can create an overly smooth film. The surface may look glossy and rich, but water has less texture to grip.

The sealer does not match the surface

A product that performs acceptably on a textured driveway may be a poor choice on a smooth pool deck or dense natural stone. Surface porosity, existing coatings, moisture exposure, traffic, and desired appearance all matter. There is no single sealer that is automatically correct for every paver surface.

No traction additive was used

Traction additives create microscopic texture in the final coat. They are especially important around pools, steps, sloped walkways, and smooth pavers. The additive must be compatible with the sealer and mixed or broadcast correctly. Too little may not help; too much can create a rough or uneven finish.

Organic film has developed

Algae, mildew, sunscreen, body oils, pollen, leaf residue, and dirt can create a slick layer even when the sealer itself is sound. In shaded Florida pool areas, this buildup can return quickly because the surface stays damp longer.

The pavers are naturally smooth

Some pavers and stone have limited texture before any coating is applied. Adding sheen can make that condition more noticeable. A low-sheen or penetrating system may be more appropriate when traction is the first priority.

How to tell whether the sealer or surface buildup is responsible

  • Slippery from day one: suspect product choice, film thickness, cure conditions, or missing traction additive.
  • Slippery only when wet: suspect smooth film build, smooth stone, or organic residue.
  • Slippery in shaded sections only: suspect algae, mildew, or moisture retention.
  • Slippery where sunscreen and pool traffic are highest: suspect surface contamination.
  • Patchy slick areas: suspect uneven application, pooling, or inconsistent cleaning.
  • Cloudy or white areas are also present: moisture may be trapped in a failing sealer film.

Do not assume another coat will improve traction. Adding more sealer over an already heavy film can make the problem worse.

Can slippery sealed pavers be fixed?

Yes, but the repair must match the cause.

When cleaning may be enough

If the surface was previously safe and the slickness developed gradually, a controlled cleaning may remove algae, sunscreen, oils, or residue. Cleaning should be strong enough to remove the film without damaging joints or weakening the existing sealer.

When a traction coat may work

If the sealer is bonded, clear, and not excessively thick, a compatible maintenance coat with the correct traction additive may improve grip. Compatibility must be confirmed first. Applying an unrelated product over an existing coating can cause whitening, poor adhesion, or peeling.

When stripping is necessary

If the surface has heavy film build, pooling, widespread whitening, peeling, or repeated incompatible coats, the failed sealer may need to be removed. Stripping is more involved than routine cleaning because the goal is to remove the coating evenly without damaging the pavers.

When the surface itself limits the result

Very smooth pavers or polished stone may remain less slip-resistant than a textured surface even after correction. In those cases, expectations, finish choice, and maintenance frequency matter as much as the product.

What not to do

  • Do not apply another glossy coat without diagnosing the current film.
  • Do not scatter loose sand over wet sealer as an improvised traction fix.
  • Do not use harsh acids or solvents without confirming the paver and sealer type.
  • Do not pressure wash aggressively enough to remove joint sand or scar the paver face.
  • Do not ignore slippery steps, slopes, coping, or pool-entry areas.

Frequently asked questions

Why are my pavers slippery only when wet?

Water exposes a lack of surface texture. The cause may be a smooth sealer film, excess coating, naturally smooth pavers, or organic residue that becomes slick when wet.

Can traction additive be added after sealing?

Sometimes. If the existing coating is sound and compatible, a properly prepared maintenance coat containing traction additive may help. It is not a reliable fix for thick, peeling, cloudy, or poorly bonded sealer.

Will pressure washing make sealed pavers less slippery?

It can help when algae or residue is responsible, but excessive pressure can damage joints and the coating. It will not correct an inherently overbuilt or incompatible sealer film.

Are wet-look sealers always slippery?

No. Slip performance depends on the surface texture, amount applied, product, traction additive, maintenance, and moisture exposure. However, high film build on smooth surfaces creates more risk than a controlled low-sheen system.

Should slippery sealer be stripped?

Stripping is usually considered when the film is too thick, uneven, peeling, whitening, or incompatible. A sound coating with minor traction concerns may have less invasive options.
