Why Are My Pavers Slippery After Sealing?

Slippery sealed pavers usually involve too much surface film, a smooth surface, insufficient traction texture, product mismatch, or contamination such as algae, sunscreen, oils, or irrigation residue.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

When the slickness started is the fastest diagnostic clue. Slippery from day one usually points to film thickness, finish choice, cure conditions, or missing traction additive. A surface that became slick months later is more likely affected by algae, sunscreen, body oils, pollen, or residue.

6 common causes of slippery sealed pavers

  1. Too much sealer: heavy or pooled film reduces usable surface texture.
  2. Smooth pavers or stone: a dense surface may have limited grip before coating.
  3. Missing or inadequate traction additive: the final coat lacks microscopic texture.
  4. Wrong product or sheen for the area: a driveway-oriented finish may not suit a wet pool deck.
  5. Algae and organic film: shaded, damp surfaces become slick over time.
  6. Sunscreen, oils, chemicals, or irrigation residue: contamination creates a slippery layer over sound sealer.

Why pool decks need different priorities

Pool decks in Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, Jacksonville Beach, and other Northeast Florida communities experience standing water, bare feet, sunscreen, furniture movement, chemicals, shade, and frequent humidity. Traction and breathability should usually outrank maximum shine.

A semi-gloss wet-look finish is not automatically unsafe, but surface texture, film thickness, additive choice, cleaning, drainage, and maintenance all affect wet traction.

What not to do

  • Do not apply another glossy coat without diagnosing the existing film.
  • Do not scatter loose sand over wet sealer as an improvised repair.
  • Do not use harsh chemicals without confirming coating compatibility.
  • Do not pressure wash aggressively enough to remove joint sand or scar pavers.
  • Do not ignore slippery steps, slopes, coping, or pool-entry areas.

Common questions

Why are sealed pavers slippery only when wet?

Water exposes limited surface texture. The cause may be heavy film, a smooth paver, missing traction additive, or algae, sunscreen, and residue that become slick when wet.

Can traction additive be added after pavers are sealed?

Sometimes. If the existing coating is sound and compatible, a properly prepared maintenance coat with traction additive may help. It is not a reliable fix for thick, peeling, cloudy, or unstable sealer.

Do slippery sealed pavers need to be stripped?

Stripping may be required when the coating is too thick, pooled, peeling, whitening, or incompatible. Cleaning or a compatible traction treatment may be possible when the film is stable.
