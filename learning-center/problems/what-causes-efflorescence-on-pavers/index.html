Paver Problems

Efflorescence forms when moisture moves through concrete pavers, dissolves naturally occurring salts, and leaves those minerals on the surface as the water evaporates.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

The direct answer

Efflorescence is not mold, paint failure, or ordinary dirt. It is a mineral deposit produced by water movement. The white or gray haze can appear on new or older pavers, especially where the base stays wet, irrigation repeatedly reaches the surface, drainage is weak, or the pavers are still releasing construction moisture.

It often looks worse after a wet period followed by hot, dry weather because evaporation pulls more dissolved salts toward the surface.

Why it is common in Florida

Northeast Florida combines heavy rain, high humidity, frequent irrigation, shallow moisture, and intense heat. Those conditions create repeated wet-and-dry cycles. Every cycle can move more mineral material through concrete pavers.

Low sections, shaded areas, driveway edges, pool-deck joints, retaining-wall bases, and areas near downspouts are common trouble spots.

How to tell efflorescence from sealer whitening

ConditionTypical appearanceWhat is happening
EfflorescencePowdery, chalky or crusty white depositMineral salts are reaching the surface
Sealer whiteningMilky or cloudy appearance inside a coatingMoisture or solvent is trapped in the sealer film
Surface residueUneven haze after cleaning or sandingCleaner, sand dust or other material remains on top

Diagnosis matters because an efflorescence treatment will not fix a failed sealer film, and adding sealer over unresolved mineral deposits can make the surface look worse.

Can pressure washing remove it?

Pressure washing may remove loose surface material, but it usually does not solve the source. Excessive pressure can also remove joint sand and damage paver edges. Established deposits normally require a purpose-made treatment, controlled dwell time, thorough rinsing, and enough drying time before any sealer is applied.

Can efflorescence come back?

Yes. Treatment removes the mineral deposit that is present that day; it does not eliminate every source of moisture or salt inside the paver system. Recurrence is more likely on newer installations, damp bases, shaded surfaces, poor-drainage zones, and areas exposed to frequent irrigation.

A responsible scope should improve the appearance without promising that efflorescence can never return.

When should pavers be sealed?

Pavers should not be sealed while active deposits remain or while the surface is still holding treatment water. The sequence is diagnosis, treatment, complete rinsing, drying, and then sealing during a stable weather window.

Frequently asked questions

Is efflorescence harmful to pavers?

It is usually an appearance problem rather than structural damage, but it can interfere with sealer adhesion and produce an uneven finish if it is sealed over.

Does efflorescence mean the pavers were installed incorrectly?

Not automatically. It can occur on correctly installed concrete products, although poor drainage and constant moisture can make it more persistent.

Will sealer hide efflorescence?

No reliable sealer should be used as a cover-up. Sealing over deposits can trap or intensify the white appearance.

How long should pavers dry after treatment?

The required time depends on weather, shade, surface density, and how much water was used. The surface must be dry throughout, not merely dry to the touch.
