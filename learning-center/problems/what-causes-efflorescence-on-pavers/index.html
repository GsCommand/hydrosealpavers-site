What Causes Efflorescence on Pavers in Florida?

Efflorescence is the white mineral residue left when moisture moves through pavers, concrete, brick, coping, walls, or bedding materials, carries dissolved salts to the surface, and evaporates.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Efflorescence requires three things: soluble salts, moving moisture, and evaporation. Remove any one of those conditions and formation slows. Treatment can remove current deposits, but it cannot guarantee they will never return while moisture continues moving through masonry.

6 common causes of paver efflorescence

  1. Salts within new concrete pavers migrate during early wet-dry cycles.
  2. Bedding sand, base material, mortar, or concrete can contain soluble minerals.
  3. Rain and irrigation repeatedly move water through the system.
  4. Poor drainage or low areas keep pavers wet longer.
  5. Ground moisture and high water tables move vapor and salts upward.
  6. Walls, coping, steps, and adjacent concrete can feed mineral-rich runoff onto pavers.

Efflorescence vs. sealer haze

ClueEfflorescenceSealer haze or failure
TexturePowdery, chalky, crystalline, or crustedMilky film, cloudy patch, flakes, or lifted coating
LocationLow areas, wall bases, irrigation paths, jointsCoated areas, pooled sealer, shaded moisture zones
Dry brushingMay reduce loose depositUsually does not remove a coating defect
RecurrenceCan return with continued moisture movementReturns if failed film or moisture condition remains
TreatmentMineral-deposit treatment suited to the surfaceCoating assessment, reworking, or removal

Where efflorescence commonly appears in Northeast Florida

  • At the bottom of sloped driveways
  • Along retaining walls, columns, steps, and coping
  • Near sprinklers and repeated irrigation runoff
  • In shaded pool-deck sections that dry slowly
  • Around downspouts, roof runoff, and low drainage areas
  • On new pavers during early moisture cycles

Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, Palm Valley, and coastal communities experience frequent rainfall, irrigation, humidity, and wet-dry cycling that support mineral migration.

What not to do

  • Do not assume every white mark is efflorescence.
  • Do not seal over visible deposits.
  • Do not use acids without testing surface and coating compatibility.
  • Do not promise permanent non-recurrence.
  • Do not ignore drainage, irrigation, or moisture sources.

Common questions

Will pressure washing remove efflorescence from pavers?

Pressure washing may remove loose powder but often does not dissolve bonded mineral deposits. A surface-appropriate treatment and thorough rinsing may be required.

Can efflorescence return after pavers are sealed?

Yes. Sealing may reduce water entry from above, but it cannot eliminate every source of moisture or salt movement from below and around the pavers.

Is efflorescence a paver-sealer failure?

Not automatically. Efflorescence is mineral residue caused by moisture carrying salts to the surface. Sealer haze is a separate coating condition, although both may occur together.
