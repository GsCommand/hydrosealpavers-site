Paver Joint-Sand Guide

Why Is Sand Coming Out of My Pavers in Jacksonville?

Joint sand can disappear because of pressure washing, concentrated runoff, shallow installation, settling, pool splash, missing edge restraint, or movement in the pavers and base.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Sand loss is not always just a sand problem. Stable pavers with shallow joints may need cleaning, proper refill, compaction, and compatible stabilization. Rocking, sinking, spreading, or edge movement must be repaired before more sand is installed.

7 common causes of joint-sand loss

  1. Pressure washing directly into joints removes sand faster than normal weathering.
  2. Roof runoff, downspouts, slopes, and irrigation concentrate moving water.
  3. Joints were never filled deeply enough and quickly appear empty.
  4. Fresh sand settled after installation because it was not compacted and topped up.
  5. Pool splash and deck cleaning move loose material toward the water.
  6. Missing or failed edge restraint allows pavers and joints to spread.
  7. Base settlement or paver movement creates widening joints that sand alone cannot repair.

How joint sand should be restored

  1. Inspect paver stability, drainage, and edge restraint.
  2. Remove debris, weeds, loose contaminated material, and failed residue.
  3. Allow joints to dry as required by the chosen system.
  4. Install the correct graded joint material to the proper depth.
  5. Compact to settle the sand through the joint.
  6. Top up low areas and clean all sand from paver faces.
  7. Apply compatible stabilization when included in the selected sealing system.

HydroSeal uses joint-sand installation or restoration language because not every project uses polymeric sand. Product and sand choice depend on the surface and system.

Jacksonville and Northeast Florida conditions

Afternoon rain, irrigation, pool splash, roof runoff, driveway slopes, tropical downpours, and frequent exterior cleaning all affect joints in Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, Jacksonville Beach, and surrounding communities. Water paths should be corrected whenever possible before joint restoration.

What not to do

  • Do not simply cover wet or contaminated joints with new sand.
  • Do not leave sand piled above joint height or across paver faces.
  • Do not expect sealer to repair rocking pavers or base settlement.
  • Do not pressure wash directly down into restored joints.
  • Do not automatically call every joint material polymeric sand.

Common questions

Can I just sweep more sand into my paver joints?

Stable, clean, dry joints may only need a proper refill and compaction. Wet, contaminated, eroded, or structurally moving joints require diagnosis and preparation first.

Does paver sealer hold joint sand in place?

Some sealing systems stabilize the upper portion of compatible joint sand, but sealer does not repair base movement, missing edge restraint, poor drainage, or rocking pavers.

Why is paver sand getting into my pool?

Low deck joints, runoff, pool splash, aggressive cleaning, unstable coping edges, or drainage paths may carry joint sand toward the pool.
