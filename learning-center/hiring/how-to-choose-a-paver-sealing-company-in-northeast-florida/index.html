How to Choose a Paver-Sealing Company in Northeast Florida

Compare the process, exclusions, materials, local experience, and accountability—not just the final number at the bottom of the quote.

Updated August 1, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Written and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team serving selected Jacksonville, St. Johns County, Duval County, and Clay County communities.

Quick answer: Choose a Northeast Florida paver-sealing company that inspects the existing coating and moisture conditions, measures the work, explains cleaning and joint-sand preparation, identifies the exact sealer system, provides written cure instructions and limitations, carries current insurance, and shows recent work on surfaces similar to yours. Do not compare quotes until the scopes match.

Paver sealing is easy to sell with a glossy before-and-after photo. It is harder to evaluate the work that determines whether the finish lasts: diagnosis, cleaning, drying, joint preparation, product compatibility, application rate, and weather judgment.

Compare scope before price

Two quotes can be hundreds or thousands of dollars apart while appearing to describe the same job. One may include deep cleaning, stain treatment, ASTM C144 kiln-dried joint sand, repairs, two controlled coats, and a multi-day schedule. Another may include a fast wash and one coat over existing conditions.

Ask each company to define the same items in writing. The professional paver-sealing scope checklist gives you a neutral comparison point.

Verify the business and its work

Confirm current insurance, claimed certifications, business identity, phone number, website, and local profile. Review recent projects on similar pavers—not only new installations or distant projects. A pool deck, travertine surface, old sealed driveway, and bare concrete paver patio require different judgment.

Reviews matter, but read them for process details. Look for comments about communication, preparation, weather decisions, cleanup, cure instructions, and how the company handled problems. A perfect star rating without detailed project evidence is less useful than a consistent body of specific local work.

Watch for these warning signs

  • A price given without measurements or meaningful inspection.
  • Promises that every stain, white spot, weed, or efflorescence issue will disappear permanently.
  • No interest in what sealer was previously applied.
  • Pressure to seal immediately despite rain, irrigation, shade, or damp joints.
  • “Best sealer” claims without discussing the surface or desired finish.
  • No written explanation of sand, coats, repairs, cure time, or exclusions.
  • Applying another coating over peeling, cloudy, or incompatible sealer without testing.

Local experience matters, but proximity is not enough

Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, Palm Valley, the Beaches, and Clay County share heat and rain, but properties differ in irrigation, shade, drainage, salt exposure, pool chemicals, traffic, and paver type. A contractor should explain the conditions at your property rather than relying on a generic “Florida-proof” process.

Make the final decision

Choose the proposal that most clearly identifies the current condition, the complete process, the materials, the limitations, the cure requirements, and who is accountable if the work does not match the written scope. Price matters, but an inexpensive incompatible coating can create a much more expensive strip-and-reseal project.

Frequently asked questions

Should I choose the lowest paver-sealing quote?

Not without comparing scope. A lower quote may exclude stain treatment, joint-sand work, repairs, drying time, multiple coats, or failed-sealer correction.

What proof should a paver-sealing contractor provide?

Verify current insurance and any claimed certification, review recent work on similar surfaces, read the written scope, and confirm that the business name, phone, website, and local profile are consistent.

What is the biggest warning sign when hiring a paver sealer?

A major warning sign is a contractor who recommends applying more sealer without inspecting moisture, old-coating compatibility, whitening, peeling, drainage, or joint condition.
