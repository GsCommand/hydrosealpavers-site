Florida Maintenance Guide

Florida Paver Maintenance Checklist

A condition-based schedule for drainage, irrigation, organic growth, stains, joint sand, sealer wear, and paver movement before small problems become restoration projects.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Walk your pavers monthly, clean when conditions require it, inspect joints and drainage seasonally, and evaluate sealer condition annually. Around the two-year mark, determine whether the surface needs maintenance, resealing, or no additional coating at all.

Maintenance schedule at a glance

Monthly 10-point checklist

  1. Walk the surface after rain and mark standing-water areas.
  2. Check irrigation overspray and leaking heads.
  3. Remove leaves, soil, and flower debris.
  4. Look for algae or mildew in shaded zones.
  5. Treat oil, rust, fertilizer, and tannin stains early.
  6. Inspect for new white powder, haze, or mineral streaks.
  7. Check steps, slopes, and pool areas for slippery buildup.
  8. Look for sand channels caused by runoff.
  9. Note rocking, sunken, spreading, or cracked pavers.
  10. Keep a photo record of changes.

Seasonal drainage and joint-sand check

  • Redirect downspouts and roof runoff away from vulnerable joints.
  • Keep pool overflow and irrigation from repeatedly washing one path.
  • Refill low joints only after the reason for sand loss is identified.
  • Repair edge restraint before spreading becomes worse.
  • Re-level unstable pavers before adding sand or sealer.

Sealer: normal wear vs. failure

Normal wearPossible failure
Even fading or reduced color enhancementPeeling, flaking, or lifting film
Gradually faster water absorptionPersistent white or milky haze
Dull traffic lanesSticky, soft, or heavily built-up coating
Minor, even joint wearWidespread sand loss or structural movement

Do not automatically add another coat over failure symptoms. Testing, cleaning, stripping, drying, or repair may be required first.

Florida conditions to manage

Properties in Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, and nearby Northeast Florida communities face strong sun, heavy rain, humidity, irrigation, leaf tannins, coastal salt, and rapid organic growth. Maintenance frequency should reflect the individual property rather than a universal calendar.

What homeowners should document

  • Sealer product and installation date
  • Joint-sand type and color
  • Areas repaired or re-leveled
  • Chemicals used for stains or cleaning
  • Photos before and after major work
  • Warranty documents and eligibility requirements

Product history helps prevent incompatible maintenance coatings and unnecessary restoration costs.

Common questions

How often should Florida pavers be professionally cleaned?

Cleaning should be based on condition rather than a rigid schedule. Shaded, irrigated, or heavily used surfaces may need attention sooner than sunny, well-drained areas.

When should paver joint sand be replaced?

Joint sand should be restored when joints are visibly low, unstable, or washing out, after the cause of the loss and any structural movement have been evaluated.

When should Florida pavers be evaluated for resealing?

Inspect sealer condition annually and consider a professional evaluation around the two-year mark. Reseal only when wear, absorption, compatibility, and surface condition support another application.
