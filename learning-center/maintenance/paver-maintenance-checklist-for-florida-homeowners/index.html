Paver Maintenance Checklist for Florida Homeowners

Florida paver maintenance should focus on drainage, irrigation, organic buildup, stains, joint sand, sealer wear, edge movement, and safe cleaning before small issues become restoration projects.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Monthly visual check

Walk the surface after rain. Look for standing water, irrigation overspray, algae, sand channels, loose edging, rocking pavers, oil, rust, tannins, and new white deposits. Early treatment is easier than waiting for stains and growth to become embedded.

Quarterly cleaning

Sweep leaves and soil, rinse light dirt, clean organic buildup with appropriate chemistry, and avoid blasting stable joints. Check downspouts, roof runoff, pool overflow, and sprinklers. Redirecting water often prevents repeated cleaning problems.

Joint and movement check

Joints should remain filled to a stable level. Refill low joints only after identifying why sand was lost. Sunken or rocking pavers need base or leveling work before sand and sealer. Edge separation should be corrected before it spreads.

Sealer inspection

Check for fading, uneven absorption, whitening, peeling, gloss buildup, and slippery areas. Schedule an evaluation around the two-year mark rather than automatically adding another coat. Keep records of the product used so future maintenance remains compatible.

Frequently asked questions

How often should pavers be pressure washed?

Only as needed. Frequent aggressive washing can remove sand and wear coatings.

When should sand be topped up?

When joints are visibly low and the cause of loss has been addressed.

When should sealer be checked?

Inspect annually and obtain a professional evaluation around two years.
