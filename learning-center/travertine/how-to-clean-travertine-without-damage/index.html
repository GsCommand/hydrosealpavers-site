Natural-Stone Cleaning Guide

How to Clean Travertine Without Damage in Florida

Travertine is calcium-based natural stone. Safe cleaning depends on stone-appropriate chemistry, controlled pressure, stain diagnosis, complete rinsing, and avoiding aggressive acids.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Clean travertine with the mildest effective stone-safe cleaner, soft agitation, controlled pressure, and thorough rinsing. Avoid vinegar, muriatic acid, and one-size-fits-all concrete cleaners because acids can etch the stone rather than clean it.

7-step travertine cleaning process

  1. Identify filled or unfilled travertine, old sealer, cracks, stains, and loose filler.
  2. Remove leaves, soil, and loose debris.
  3. Protect plants, metals, glass, pool equipment, and adjacent finishes.
  4. Test a stone-safe cleaner in an inconspicuous area.
  5. Apply evenly and prevent the chemistry from drying.
  6. Agitate gently and rinse with controlled pressure.
  7. Allow complete drying before evaluating stains, repairs, or sealing.

Pressure-washing precautions

  • Use a wide fan and greater distance than on durable concrete.
  • Do not aim directly into holes, cracks, grout, coping joints, or soft filler.
  • Keep equipment moving to avoid stripes and etched paths.
  • Use a surface cleaner only with suitable nozzles, flow, pace, and testing.
  • Do not rely on pressure alone for stains that require chemistry.

What permanently damages travertine

Acid etching, aggressive pressure, enlarged pores, lost filler, scratches, and some deep stains are changes to the stone—not dirt sitting on top. Cleaning may improve appearance, but complete restoration cannot be promised before inspection.

Northeast Florida conditions

Travertine pool decks in Nocatee, Ponte Vedra Beach, Palm Valley, and Jacksonville-area communities often collect algae, sunscreen, leaf tannins, irrigation minerals, and coastal residue. Shade and humidity increase dwell time for organic growth.

Common questions

Can vinegar be used to clean travertine?

No. Vinegar is acidic and can etch calcium-based travertine, dull the finish, damage filler, and permanently change the stone.

Can travertine be pressure washed?

Yes, with controlled pressure, a wide spray pattern, proper distance, and care around holes, filler, grout, coping edges, and existing sealer. Aggressive close-range pressure can damage the stone.

Why does travertine look chalky after cleaning?

Possible causes include acid etching, mineral residue, weathering, old coating, cleaner residue, or moisture. Diagnosis is needed before more chemistry or sealer is applied.
