How to Clean Travertine Without Damage

Clean travertine with pH-appropriate stone-safe products, soft agitation, controlled pressure, thorough rinsing, and test areas. Avoid aggressive acids and close-range pressure.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Why travertine needs different care

Travertine is a calcium-based natural stone. Acidic cleaners can etch the surface, dull the finish, enlarge pores, and damage filler. Even products safe for concrete pavers may be too aggressive for stone.

Routine cleaning

Remove loose debris, pre-wet surrounding plants, apply a stone-safe cleaner, allow controlled dwell time, gently agitate, and rinse thoroughly. For organic growth, use a product and dilution approved for natural stone. Do not let chemistry dry.

Pressure washing precautions

Use lower aggression, a wide fan, and greater distance. Avoid aiming directly into holes, cracks, grout, coping edges, or soft filler. A surface cleaner can be used only with appropriate nozzles and technique.

Stains and expectations

Oil, rust, tannins, hard water, and metal runoff require different treatments. Some stains penetrate deeply and need poultice work or multiple treatments. Permanent etching or mineral change cannot be cleaned away because the stone itself has changed.

Frequently asked questions

Can vinegar clean travertine?

No. Vinegar is acidic and can etch calcium-based stone.

Can bleach be used?

Only in stone-safe dilution and with careful rinsing; it is not appropriate for every stain.

Why does travertine look chalky?

Etching, mineral residue, weathering, or old coating may create a chalky appearance.
