Paver Joint-Sand Comparison

ASTM C144 Sand vs. Polymeric Sand: Which Is Better for Pavers?

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on joint width and depth, drainage, base stability, installation method, repair needs, planned sealer, and tolerance for activation or residue risk.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

ASTM C144 kiln-dried sand is often a strong choice when the project uses a compatible sand-stabilizing sealer and future repairability matters. Polymeric sand can provide independent joint binding when correctly installed and activated. Neither material fixes base movement, poor drainage, missing edge restraint, or improperly prepared joints.

7 factors that matter more than the label

  1. Clean joint sidewalls
  2. Correct joint depth
  3. Proper gradation for the joint width
  4. Mechanical compaction and topping up
  5. Stable base and edge restraint
  6. Drainage and runoff control
  7. Compatibility with the full sealing or stabilization system

When ASTM C144 sand fits

HydroSeal commonly uses kiln-dried ASTM C144 masonry sand in appropriate paver-restoration projects where clean joints, compaction, a compatible stabilizing sealer, and future repairability are priorities. The exact sand color and system should match the project; joint material should not automatically be described as polymeric sand.

When polymeric sand fits

Polymeric sand may fit projects designed around independent water-activated joint binding, provided the pavers are dry, the joints meet depth and width requirements, residue is removed completely, watering is controlled, and dry weather supports curing. Manufacturer instructions govern the installation.

What neither sand can repair

  • Rocking or sunken pavers
  • Spreading caused by failed edge restraint
  • Base erosion or washout
  • Large uncontrolled gaps
  • Drainage concentrating water through joints
  • A failed coating trapping sand or residue

Common questions

Is ASTM C144 sand the same as regular play sand?

No. ASTM C144 is a grading specification for masonry sand. The actual material still needs to be clean, dry, appropriately graded, and suitable for the paver joints and stabilization system.

Does polymeric sand always last longer?

No. Performance depends on joint depth, cleaning, compaction, watering, drainage, edge restraint, weather, and installation quality.

Can polymeric sand be used under paver sealer?

Sometimes, but manufacturer compatibility, activation, curing, and moisture requirements must be followed. It should not be assumed that every combination is compatible.
