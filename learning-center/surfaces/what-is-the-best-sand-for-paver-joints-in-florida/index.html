Paver Joint-Sand Guide

What Is the Best Sand for Paver Joints in Florida?

The best joint sand depends on joint width and depth, drainage, base stability, installation method, repair needs, planned sealer, and whether the system relies on water-activated polymer or a compatible stabilizing sealer.

Written by HydroSeal Editorial Team · Field reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified team · Technically reviewed August 1, 2026

Quick answer

For many Florida paver-restoration projects, clean kiln-dried sand meeting an appropriate ASTM C144 grading can work well when it is properly compacted and stabilized with a compatible sealer. Polymeric sand can also perform well when the joints, weather, watering, drainage, and cure conditions meet the manufacturer’s requirements. Neither option repairs base failure, movement, or concentrated runoff.

ASTM C144 sand versus polymeric sand

FactorASTM C144 kiln-dried sandPolymeric sand
How it stabilizesCompaction plus the compatible stabilization or sealing systemWater activates a polymer binder

Residue riskLoose grains must be removed before sealingUnremoved binder can leave polymeric haze
RepairabilityGenerally easier to reopen and refill during future repairsHardened sections can be more difficult to remove cleanly
Common failureWashout where joints are shallow, loose, or exposed to concentrated runoffIncomplete activation, cracking, washout, or haze from poor installation

Seven factors that matter more than the product name

  1. Clean joint sidewalls
  2. Correct joint depth
  3. Gradation suitable for the joint width
  4. Mechanical compaction and topping up
  5. Stable paver base and edge restraint
  6. Drainage and roof-runoff control
  7. Compatibility with the complete sealing or stabilization system

HydroSeal’s field approach

HydroSeal commonly uses kiln-dried ASTM C144 masonry sand on appropriate restoration projects where clean joints, controlled compaction, a compatible joint-stabilizing sealer, and future repairability are priorities. The final choice still depends on the existing materials, drainage, paver movement, joint dimensions, and coating history.

When polymeric sand may be appropriate

Polymeric sand may suit projects designed around independent water-activated binding when the pavers and joints are dry, the joint dimensions meet the product instructions, all residue can be removed, watering can be controlled, and the forecast provides enough dry curing time. Manufacturer instructions govern the installation.

What joint sand cannot fix

  • Sunken or rocking pavers
  • Failed or missing edge restraint
  • Base erosion
  • Drainage directing water through the joints
  • Large uncontrolled gaps
  • A failed coating trapping moisture or residue

Common questions

Is ASTM C144 sand the same as play sand?

No. ASTM C144 is a grading specification for masonry aggregate. The material still needs to be clean, dry, appropriately graded, and suitable for the joint system.

Does polymeric sand always last longer?

No. Longevity depends heavily on joint preparation, depth, compaction, watering, drainage, weather, and edge restraint.

Can polymeric sand be used with paver sealer?

Sometimes. Compatibility, activation, cure time, and moisture requirements from both manufacturers must be followed.
