How to Clean Pavers Without Damaging Them

The safest process uses inspection, stain-specific chemistry, controlled pressure, complete rinsing, and protection for joints, coatings, plants, metals, and nearby surfaces.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Use the mildest effective process. Identify the paver and stain, treat the contaminant with the correct cleaner, use pressure mainly for controlled rinsing, protect the joints, and allow complete drying before sanding or sealing.

The 7-step safe-cleaning process

  1. Inspect: identify material, sealer, stains, cracks, low joints, drainage, plants, paint, glass, and metal.
  2. Dry clean: remove leaves, loose dirt, and debris.
  3. Protect: pre-wet plants and isolate sensitive surfaces and runoff paths.
  4. Treat by stain: organic growth, oil, rust, tannins, and efflorescence need different chemistry.
  5. Agitate and dwell: follow product directions and prevent cleaner from drying.
  6. Rinse with controlled pressure: use consistent distance, movement, and overlap.
  7. Inspect and dry: verify the surface and joints before repair, sand, or sealer.

6 mistakes that cause damage

  1. Using one harsh cleaner for every stain.
  2. Holding a narrow nozzle too close.
  3. Blasting directly into joints.
  4. Allowing chemicals to dry on the surface.
  5. Mixing bleach with acids, ammonia, or unknown products.
  6. Sealing before the pavers and joints are fully dry.

Local Florida conditions

Humidity, irrigation, afternoon rain, shade, tree debris, and coastal exposure make cleaning more frequent in Jacksonville, Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, and Jacksonville Beach. These conditions make correct chemistry and drying windows more important—not stronger pressure.

Common questions

Can bleach be used on pavers?

Diluted sodium hypochlorite can treat organic growth on many concrete pavers, but it is not suitable for every stain, sealer, natural stone, plant area, or adjacent material.

Can pressure washing remove paver sealer?

It may disturb weak or peeling sealer, but pressure alone does not reliably strip a sound coating and can leave an uneven result.

How long should pavers dry before sealing?

Drying time depends on weather, shade, joint depth, drainage, and surface material. The pavers and joints must be fully dry enough for the selected product before sealing.
