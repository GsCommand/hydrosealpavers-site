How to Remove Algae and Mildew From Pavers

Successful cleaning treats the living growth, allows controlled dwell time, rinses safely, and reduces the shade, irrigation, drainage, and debris conditions that cause rapid return.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Do not rely on pressure alone. Remove loose debris, protect plants and metals, apply a compatible organic-growth treatment, keep it wet for the required dwell time, rinse thoroughly, and correct recurring moisture where practical.

Why algae and mildew return

  1. Shade keeps the surface wet longer.
  2. Irrigation repeatedly feeds moisture.
  3. Low areas and poor drainage hold water.
  4. Leaves, pollen, sunscreen, and soil provide nutrients.
  5. Porous joints and pavers retain moisture.
  6. Screen enclosures and walls reduce airflow.
  7. Florida humidity shortens drying cycles.

The 6-step removal process

  1. Sweep leaves and loose debris.
  2. Pre-wet and protect landscaping, metals, paint, glass, and pool equipment.
  3. Apply the correct diluted treatment evenly.
  4. Allow controlled dwell without letting the product dry.
  5. Agitate difficult areas and rinse with moderate, consistent pressure.
  6. Reinspect after drying before sealing or applying another treatment.

What not to do

  • Never mix bleach with acids, ammonia, or unknown cleaners.
  • Do not increase concentration simply to shorten dwell time.
  • Do not blast joints and edges with a narrow nozzle.
  • Do not seal over living growth or cleaner residue.
  • Do not promise permanent prevention while moisture conditions remain.

Local recurrence patterns

Shaded pool decks and walkways in Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, Palm Valley, and Jacksonville Beach often experience repeated wetting, humidity, salt air, vegetation, and screen-enclosure shade. Cleaning removes current growth; prevention requires moisture and maintenance changes.

Common questions

Will vinegar remove algae from pavers?

Vinegar may affect light organic growth, but acids can react with concrete, natural stone, coatings, and nearby materials. It is not a universal paver cleaner.

Can pavers be sealed immediately after algae cleaning?

No. The surface must be completely clean, thoroughly rinsed, and dry enough for the selected sealer, including moisture held in the joints.

Does paver sealer stop algae and mildew?

Sealer may reduce absorption and make maintenance easier, but algae can still grow on moisture, dirt, pollen, and organic residue sitting on top of a sealed surface.
