How to Remove Algae and Mildew From Pavers

Remove algae and mildew by treating the organic growth, allowing controlled dwell time, rinsing thoroughly, and correcting the moisture conditions that allow rapid return.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Why it returns

Shade, irrigation overspray, poor drainage, tree debris, humidity, and porous pavers keep surfaces damp. Cleaning removes growth, but it does not change the environment. North-facing walkways, screen enclosures, pool decks, and areas under trees usually need more frequent maintenance.

Treatment process

Sweep loose debris first. Protect plants and metals, pre-wet sensitive areas, apply the cleaning solution evenly, prevent it from drying, and rinse completely. Use pressure to assist rinsing, not as the only treatment. Blasting without killing growth can leave roots and spores in pores and joints.

Avoid common mistakes

Do not mix bleach with acids, ammonia, or unknown cleaners. Do not use excessive concentration to save time. Strong solution can discolor adjacent materials and damage vegetation. Do not seal active algae; the surface must be clean, rinsed, and completely dry.

Prevention

Redirect sprinklers, improve drainage, trim vegetation where practical, remove leaf debris, and schedule light maintenance before heavy buildup develops. Sealer can reduce absorption and make cleaning easier, but it does not make a shaded wet surface maintenance-free.

Frequently asked questions

Will vinegar remove algae?

It may affect light growth, but acids can react with concrete and are not a universal cleaner.

Can I seal immediately after cleaning?

No. The pavers and joints need complete dry time.

Does sealer stop algae?

It may reduce absorption, but algae can still grow on dirt and moisture sitting on top.
