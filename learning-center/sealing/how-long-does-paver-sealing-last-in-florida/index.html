How Long Does Paver Sealing Last in Florida?

Two to three years is a practical planning range for many professionally sealed Florida surfaces, but exposure, traffic, drainage, moisture, product choice, and preparation can move that number in either direction.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Most professionally sealed Florida pavers should be evaluated around the two-year mark and commonly last about two to three years. A full-sun Jacksonville driveway may wear sooner than a covered patio, while coastal properties can require earlier evaluation.

Typical lifespan by surface

SurfacePlanning rangeMain wear factors
DrivewayAbout 2–3 yearsSun, traffic, hot tires, oil, irrigation
Pool deckAbout 2–3 yearsWater, chemicals, furniture, wet traffic
Covered patioOften longer visuallyShade, organic buildup, slower drying
Coastal areaMay need earlier evaluationSalt, wind-driven moisture, frequent wetting

8 factors that determine lifespan

  1. Cleaning and surface preparation
  2. Moisture at application
  3. Old-sealer compatibility
  4. Correct coverage rate
  5. Sun and heat exposure
  6. Vehicle and foot traffic
  7. Irrigation, drainage, and standing water
  8. Cleaning chemicals and pressure-washing habits

How Northeast Florida conditions change the range

Jacksonville driveways often experience intense sun, heat, irrigation, and vehicle traffic. Nocatee, Ponte Vedra, and Palm Valley properties may combine irrigation, humidity, shade, and large pool decks. Jacksonville Beach and Ponte Vedra Beach can add salt and wind-driven moisture.

Common questions

Can paver sealer last five years in Florida?

Sheltered, lightly used areas may retain protection that long, but five years is not a reliable expectation for exposed Florida driveways or pool decks.

Does applying more sealer make it last longer?

No. Excessive film build can trap moisture, become slippery, whiten, or peel and may fail sooner.

Should Florida pavers be resealed every year?

Usually not. Annual recoating can create unnecessary buildup. Evaluate condition instead.
