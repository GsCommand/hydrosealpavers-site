How Long Does Paver Sealing Last in Florida?

In Florida, a professional paver-sealing job commonly lasts about two to three years, but exposure, drainage, traffic, product choice, and preparation can shorten or extend that range.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

The realistic lifespan

Two to three years is a useful planning range for many Northeast Florida driveways and pool decks. Full-sun driveways, heavy vehicle traffic, standing irrigation water, salt exposure, and repeated pressure washing usually shorten the cycle. Covered patios and lightly used walkways often hold their appearance longer.

What determines how long it lasts

Surface preparation is the biggest variable. Sealer applied over dirt, algae, trapped moisture, efflorescence, or unstable old coating can fail early. Application rate also matters: too little leaves uneven protection; too much can create whitening, slipperiness, or peeling.

Signs it is time to reseal

Look for faded color, reduced stain resistance, sand loss, uneven absorption, dull traffic lanes, or water soaking in quickly. Do not wait for widespread peeling or whitening, which may mean the old coating needs removal rather than a maintenance coat.

How to extend the lifespan

Keep irrigation off the pavers when possible, clean organic buildup before it becomes embedded, avoid harsh acids and unknown degreasers, and use moderate pressure. Do not apply store-bought sealer over a professional system without confirming compatibility.

Frequently asked questions

Can sealer last five years?

Sheltered areas may retain protection that long, but five years is not a reliable expectation for exposed Florida driveways or pool decks.

Does more sealer last longer?

No. Excessive film build can fail sooner.

Should pavers be resealed every year?

Usually not. Annual recoating can create buildup.
