Paver Sealing

How to Choose the Right Paver Sealer for Your Home

The right paver sealer depends on the surface, existing coating, desired appearance, traction needs, drainage, and Florida weather—not on which container promises the highest gloss.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Start with the surface

Concrete pavers, clay brick, and travertine do not behave the same way. Pool decks require more attention to wet traction. Driveways require hot-tire and stain resistance. A shaded patio may need better breathability because it dries slowly.

Identify what is already on the pavers

Product compatibility is critical. A new coating must bond to the existing system or the old coating must be removed. Guessing can cause softening, whitening, peeling, or uneven gloss. If history is unknown, testing is safer than assuming.

Choose the finish deliberately

Natural-look products provide protection with less color change. Enhancing or wet-look products deepen color and can add sheen. High gloss is not automatically more durable and can highlight uneven pavers or increase traction concerns.

Breathability and Florida moisture

Pavers release moisture from below. A breathable system helps vapor movement, but no product can compensate for sealing a damp surface or ignoring drainage. Rain, humidity, irrigation, shade, and dew must be considered.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best sealer?

There is no universal best product. The best choice matches the surface, old coating, finish, traffic, and moisture.

Is high gloss better?

No. Gloss is an appearance choice.

Can one sealer cover travertine and concrete?

Sometimes, but it should never be assumed.
