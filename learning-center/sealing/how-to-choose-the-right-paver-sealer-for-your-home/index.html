How to Choose the Right Paver Sealer for Your Home

The right sealer is determined by the surface, existing coating, moisture, drainage, traffic, traction needs, and desired finish—not by which container promises the most shine.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Choose a paver sealer only after matching the product to the surface and conditions. Concrete pavers, clay brick, travertine, pool decks, and previously sealed surfaces do not all need the same system. The safest decision starts with compatibility and moisture—not gloss level.

7 factors that determine the right paver sealer

  1. Surface material: concrete pavers, clay brick, travertine, and natural stone absorb differently.
  2. Existing coating: new sealer must bond to the old system or the old coating may need removal.
  3. Desired appearance: choose between a semi-gloss wet-look finish and a natural or nearly invisible finish.
  4. Moisture and drainage: shade, irrigation, standing water, dew, and vapor movement affect performance.
  5. Traffic: driveways need hot-tire and wear resistance; pool decks need wet-traction planning.
  6. Joint condition: decide whether joint-sand stabilization is part of the goal.
  7. Maintenance expectations: visible film finishes and penetrating sealers age differently.

Wet-look versus natural-look

A semi-gloss wet-look finish deepens color and creates a clean, noticeable sheen. HydroSeal uses Trident Hurricane CAT 5 in appropriate applications when color enhancement, surface protection, and joint-sand stabilization are desired.

A natural-look penetrating system protects with less visible change. HydroSeal uses Trident Break Wall in appropriate applications when breathability, water and salt resistance, and preservation of the original appearance are priorities. These products are examples of systems HydroSeal uses—not universal recommendations for every surface.

Compatibility is more important than brand name

Applying a new coating over an incompatible or failing old sealer can cause softening, whitening, peeling, blotchy color, or poor adhesion. If the product history is unknown, a test area or removal assessment is safer than guessing. Another coat does not repair an unstable layer underneath it.

Florida moisture changes the decision

Northeast Florida pavers can hold moisture below the surface even when the top appears dry. Irrigation, humidity, afternoon storms, shade, poor drainage, and overnight dew all matter. A breathable product can help vapor movement, but no sealer can compensate for damp installation conditions or unresolved drainage.

Common questions

What is the best paver sealer?

There is no universal best paver sealer. The right product depends on the surface, existing coating, moisture, traffic, traction needs, desired appearance, and maintenance plan.

Is a high-gloss paver sealer better?

No. Gloss is mainly an appearance choice. More shine does not automatically mean better durability and may increase visible wear or traction concerns.

Can the same sealer be used on concrete pavers and travertine?

Sometimes, but it should never be assumed. Travertine and concrete pavers have different absorption, moisture, and finish requirements, so product compatibility should be confirmed first.
