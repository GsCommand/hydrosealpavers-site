Florida Paver Maintenance

How Often Should Pavers Be Sealed in Florida?

Most Florida pavers should be inspected around every two years and resealed only when the coating, absorption, joint sand, appearance, and exposure show that another application is appropriate.

Published and reviewed by HydroSeal’s Trident Master Certified paver-sealing team · Updated July 30, 2026

Quick answer

Inspect Florida pavers about every two years, but do not automatically add another coat. Some exposed driveways may need attention sooner, while protected patios may perform longer. Peeling, whitening, moisture, or incompatible old coating must be corrected before resealing.

Recommended inspection timing by surface

SurfaceWhen to inspectWhat to check
DrivewayAbout every 2 yearsTraffic lanes, stains, sand loss, faded enhancement
Pool deckEvery 18–24 monthsTraction, chemical wear, water exposure, furniture marks
Covered patioAbout every 2–3 yearsOrganic buildup, absorption, shade-related moisture
TravertineCondition-basedAbsorption, salt exposure, breathability, appearance

These are inspection intervals, not promises that every surface needs resealing at that exact time.

7 signs resealing may be appropriate

  1. Water absorbs more quickly than before.
  2. Color enhancement has faded evenly.
  3. Traffic areas look dull.
  4. Joint sand is beginning to loosen or erode.
  5. Stains penetrate more easily.
  6. The surface is clean, dry, stable, and compatible with the planned system.
  7. The existing coating is worn—not peeling, whitening, soft, or heavily built up.

When not to reseal

  • Peeling or delaminating coating
  • White haze or trapped moisture
  • Heavy efflorescence or mineral residue
  • Oil or silicone contamination
  • Unknown coating chemistry
  • Standing water or unresolved drainage
  • A surface that is still performing well and does not need more film

Adding product over a failed or incompatible layer can make restoration more expensive.

Why annual resealing can backfire

More sealer does not automatically mean more protection. Recoating too often can create heavy film, uneven sheen, visible wear lines, slipperiness, whitening, and peeling. A maintenance plan should preserve the existing system rather than continually bury it.

A practical Florida maintenance cycle

  1. Clean organic buildup as needed with surface-appropriate methods.
  2. Keep irrigation from repeatedly soaking the pavers.
  3. Monitor joint-sand loss and drainage.
  4. Document the installed product.
  5. Inspect around the two-year mark.
  6. Test compatibility before changing products.
  7. Reseal only when the existing condition supports it.

Common questions

Is resealing every two years mandatory?

No. Two years is a useful inspection interval, not an automatic recoating rule. The existing sealer and surface condition should determine the timing.

Can pavers be resealed every year?

Annual resealing is usually unnecessary and can create excess coating buildup, uneven sheen, slipperiness, or moisture problems.

Do pool decks need resealing more often than driveways?

Sometimes. Pool decks experience frequent wetting, chemicals, furniture movement, and wet foot traffic, while driveways experience vehicle wear, hot tires, and oil. Each surface should be inspected individually.
