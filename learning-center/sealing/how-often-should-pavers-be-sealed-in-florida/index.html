How Often Should Pavers Be Sealed in Florida?

Most Florida pavers should be evaluated every two years and resealed only when wear, absorption, sand stability, and the existing coating show that another application is needed.

Updated July 30, 2026 · HydroSeal field guidance

Use inspection, not a blind schedule

A fixed annual schedule is too aggressive for many surfaces, while waiting four or five years may allow color loss, sand erosion, stains, and uneven wear. The practical approach is an inspection around the two-year mark.

Typical timing by surface

A high-traffic driveway may need attention sooner than a covered patio. Pool decks need closer monitoring because of water, chemicals, furniture, and slip-safety concerns. Coastal exposure, heavy shade, poor drainage, and frequent irrigation can accelerate wear.

When not to reseal

Do not reseal over peeling, whitening, trapped moisture, heavy efflorescence, oily contamination, or unknown incompatible coatings. Adding product over a failed layer usually locks in the problem. If the surface is still performing well, another coat may be unnecessary.

A sensible maintenance plan

Clean as needed, keep joints filled, correct drainage problems, and document the product used. Reinspect after major pressure washing, construction work, or chemical exposure. Product history can prevent an expensive compatibility mistake.

Frequently asked questions

Is every two years mandatory?

No. It is an inspection interval, not an automatic recoating rule.

Can I wait until sealer is gone?

You can, but waiting too long may allow staining and sand loss.

Does a pool deck need it more often?

Sometimes, because wetting and chemical exposure increase wear.
